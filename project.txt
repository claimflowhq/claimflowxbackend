1. Project Overview
ClaimFlow is a blockchain-powered insurance claims management platform built on Stellar that enables claimants to submit insurance claims, authorized reviewers to assess and approve or reject claims, and administrators to track payout authorization and transaction activity.
The platform addresses common challenges in insurance claims processing, including fragmented claim records, limited visibility into claim progress, inconsistent approval procedures, and difficulty verifying whether a claim has been approved or authorized for payment.
With ClaimFlow, a claimant submits a claim containing the relevant policy reference, incident description, and requested compensation amount.
The system generates a unique claim reference and makes the submission available to authorized reviewers.
Reviewers can assess the claim, record their decisions, and update its status.
A Soroban smart contract maintains verifiable records of important claim-state changes and enforces authorization rules, ensuring that only permitted reviewers can approve claims or authorize payouts.
The payout dashboard provides visibility into approved claims, authorized payout amounts, and relevant Stellar Testnet transactions.
Primary MVP goal: Build a functional claims management platform demonstrating claim submission, structured review, Soroban-based claim-state management, authorized payout decisions, and transaction monitoring.
Important scope distinction: Claim approval and payout authorization are not the same as transferring funds. The core MVP must demonstrate secure payout authorization. Actual USDC settlement is optional and should be implemented only if separately agreed within the sprint.

Smart contract security rules
Every claim must have a unique identifier.
Only authorized wallets can perform protected reviewer actions.
Claims must follow predefined state transitions.
Rejected claims cannot receive payout authorization.
Unreviewed claims cannot be authorized for payout.
Approved claim amounts must be valid and nonnegative.
Authorized payout amounts cannot exceed approved amounts.
A claim cannot receive duplicate payout authorization.
Unauthorized wallets cannot alter approval decisions.
Failed contract transactions must not incorrectly update claim state.
Recommended contract data model
The contract should maintain only essential references and authorization information:
Claim ID.
Claimant wallet or pseudonymous identifier.
Reviewer authorization reference.
Claim status.
Approved amount.
Payout authorization status.
Relevant timestamps or ledger references.
Contract events.
Personal medical details, incident descriptions, insurance documents, and private claimant records should not be stored directly on-chain.
Acceptance criteria: The contract can register claims, enforce authorized state transitions, record reviewer decisions, and authorize payouts only for eligible approved claims.

12. Definition of Done
The ClaimFlow MVP will be considered complete when a claimant can submit an insurance claim, receive a unique reference, and track its status through a functional interface.
Authorized reviewers must be able to assess claims, approve or reject submissions, and record their decisions.
The Soroban contract must enforce valid claim-state transitions, restrict protected actions to authorized reviewers, and prevent duplicate payout authorizations.
The payout dashboard must distinguish between approved claims, authorized payouts, and actual payment settlements, with relevant on-chain transaction references.
The team must provide automated contract tests, transaction evidence, a deployed MVP, and technical documentation.
Development priority: Focus on the complete claims lifecycle: claim submission → reviewer assessment → approval or rejection → Soroban state update → payout authorization → transaction monitoring. Actual insurance settlement and advanced insurance operations should remain outside the initial sprint.
